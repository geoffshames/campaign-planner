Album Marketing Plan

Temporex

Fantastic Machines
Prepared for Independent Co. · $14,000 media budget · Jul–Nov 2026

Temporex comes into this album with about 1.4M Spotify monthly listeners, mostly catalog, and a social footprint a fraction of that size. That gap is a conversion problem, not an awareness one. This is the digital plan that converts it across four singles into Fantastic Machines: paid media behind the strongest asset on every drop, DSP conversion (pre-save, Marquee, Showcase, and an album Countdown that opens at Real Time), and Strive FM rideshare playlisting as the album's streaming hedge. The $14K below is what CCD deploys, drop by drop.

What we're working with

Ababa

The animated world

The Fantastic Machines universe, the gnome, the surreal cartoon look, is produced by Ababa (Dawit's studio) on the production line. Every single ships an animated coming-soon / pre-save asset from Ababa; the album adds the explorable Blender worlds that feed the Machine Room game.

2 videos

The music videos

Real Time is the in-house video; Fantastic Machine is the big-budget title track. Each premieres and is cut into verticals that run through the drop.

Hero

The live footage

The live set is the hero footage, cut into a library of vertical clips that seed organic across the whole rollout, with the best performers amplified paid.

Content calendar

The rollout, post by post

Every post across the cycle, on its day, with the asset it uses and the CTA. Every release date is locked; the posts in between follow the fixed 14-day cadence.

The repeating arc

Every single runs the same 14-day arc: T-14 pre-save + single-art announce, then a teaser a couple of days later, then a harder pre-save hit, then release-day out-now hero plus Spotify Marquee, then paid amplifies the winners a couple of days after (run dark), then a live clip points back two weeks after release, then straight into the next single. The album is the exception: its tail keeps pushing 4 to 8 weeks, not two.

Posting rules
  • Cap organic at 1 to 2 posts a week. Paid carries the frequency, so you never have to over-post.
  • Post midday-ish, 1 to 2 hours before your Instagram-reported peak, so social proof builds into the peak window. Skip early-morning slots while U.S. fans are asleep.
  • On every release day, all pre-save CTAs flip to listen-now / stream-now.
  • Boost the organic winners to your existing followers to convert the ~90% the post did not reach (Water Holes’ organic announce did ~900 likes and ~300 pre-saves on its own).
Where spend goes

Meta is heaviest overall. The non-video singles (Water Holes, Somewhere I’m Better Now) carry the higher Meta budget; the music-video singles (Real Time, Fantastic Machine) shift spend to YouTube. Spotify Marquee + Showcase are held for release moments. Strive FM rideshare (~$1.6K / ~150K streams) sits on the album as the passive streaming backbone. TikTok runs top-funnel. ~$2K is held in reserve to double down on whatever overperforms.

Creative activations

Three greenlight-later plays

World-building that captures owned audience. Pitch concepts, not costed against the $14K.

Web game

The Machine Room

A browser-playable room built from the album's Ababa Blender assets. Players parkour through the Fantastic Machines world; in-world billboards read “new single, out now.” A gamified gate (“your phone number is your ticket to the ride”) drops email / phone into Laylo.

Why. Converts curiosity into an owned email / SMS list and reuses assets we're already building. Clip the playthrough and it doubles as the album's dark-ride content.
Custom microsite

Archive: the Machine repair manual

A standalone site framed as a repair manual for a machine that never existed. Each page is one illustrated component with a made-up name; new parts get added across the rollout, and the full machine only reveals on album day.

Why. A collectible artifact fans share, the modern liner notes. Generates inbound press and becomes the destination link from every drop.
IRL booth

Collaborative Animation Booth

A booth at a live activation. Heavy Manners, the LA animation space Joseph teaches at, is a natural fit, alongside adjacent animated-shorts events. Attendees draw one component of the machine on an iPad; each drawing joins a growing master illustration, and by album day the machine is fully assembled, built by strangers.

Why. Turns every event into a content moment and a fan artifact. “Your drawing made it into the film” converts attendees into superfans.
