Album Marketing Plan

Temporex

Fantastic Machines
Prepared for Independent Co. · $14,000 media budget · Jul–Nov 2026

Temporex comes into this album with about 1.4M Spotify monthly listeners, mostly catalog, and a social footprint a fraction of that size. That gap is a conversion problem, not an awareness one. This is the digital plan that converts it across four singles into the album Fantastic Machines: paid media behind the strongest asset on every drop, DSP conversion (per-single pre-save, Marquee, Showcase, and an album Countdown that opens at Real Time), Strive FM rideshare playlisting as the album's streaming hedge, and three world-building activations that capture owned audience. The $14K below is what CCD deploys, drop by drop.

How it runs

The content engine is the live set filmed at the show as the hero footage, cut into a library of vertical clips that seed organic across the whole rollout, plus two music videos: Real Time (in-house) and the big-budget Fantastic Machine title track. Each single follows the same rhythm below, with a per-track Spotify Canvas (a 9:16 loop, no text) shipping with every drop.

Creative activations

Three greenlight-later plays

World-building that captures owned audience. Pitch concepts, not costed against the $14K.

Web game

The Machine Room

A browser-playable room built from the album's Blender assets. Players walk and parkour through the Fantastic Machines world; billboards along the route read “new single, out now,” FIFA-pitch-side style, so it never feels like an ad over the animation. A gamified gate unlocks the next room: “your phone number is your ticket to the ride,” dropping email / phone straight into Laylo.

Why. The best answer to the core problem: it converts curiosity into an owned email / SMS list, and it reuses assets we're already building. Clip the playthrough and it doubles as the album's dark-ride content.
Custom microsite

Archive: the Machine repair manual

A standalone site framed as a repair manual for a machine that never existed. Each page is one component, illustrated, with a made-up technical name and an unknown function. New parts get added across the rollout; the full machine is never shown until album day. Dr. Seuss meets Looney Toons.

Why. A collectible artifact fans share, the modern liner notes. Generates inbound press and becomes the destination link from every drop.
IRL booth

Collaborative Animation Booth

A booth at the Heavy Manners show where attendees draw one component of the machine on an iPad. Each drawing joins a growing master illustration; the booth prints a personalized flier with the current machine and their piece in it. By album day the machine is fully assembled, built by strangers.

Why. Turns every event into a content moment, a fan artifact, and a real piece of the album world. “Your drawing made it into the film” converts attendees into superfans.
