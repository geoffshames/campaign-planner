Crowd Control Digital · Album Marketing Plan

Temporex / Fantastic Machines

Four singles cascading into the album. A single every ~4 weeks.
Prepared for Independent Co. · $14,000 media budget · July 2026

Temporex comes into this album with about 1.4M Spotify monthly listeners, mostly catalog, and a social footprint a fraction of that size. That gap is a conversion problem, not an awareness one. This is the digital plan that converts it across four singles into the album Fantastic Machines: paid media behind the strongest asset on every drop, DSP conversion (per-single pre-save, Marquee, Showcase, and an album Countdown that opens at Single 2), Strive FM rideshare playlisting as the album's streaming hedge, and three world-building activations that capture owned audience. The $14K below is what CCD deploys, drop by drop.

How it works

Every single runs the same rhythm: pre-save live at D-14, the animated coming-soon asset at D-7, out-now on drop day, then live cutdowns through D+14. Deliverable base per single: 15s + 30s cutdowns in three CTA versions (vertical + square), a per-track Spotify Canvas (a 9:16 loop, no text), and the live footage from the 19th. The two video singles add a YouTube premiere and MV cutdowns. Below, each single is broken out with its own schedule, deliverables and spend.

Live performance · the hero content

The live set filmed on the 19th is the hero content. Luis cuts it into ~250 vertical clips. Those run as the primary organic content on Reels and TikTok across the whole rollout; the best performers get paid amplification on Meta and TikTok; the full set goes to YouTube around release.

The two music videos

Two music videos. Real Time (Single 2) is Joseph + Luis in-house: a YouTube premiere of the full cut plus 8–12 vertical cutdowns to organic, paid and Shorts. Fantastic Machine (Single 4) is the big-budget title track: a YouTube premiere as the tentpole, 15–20 cutdowns, the biggest paid push of the campaign, and the lead-in to the album.

Creative activations

Three greenlight-later plays

World-building that captures owned audience. Pitch concepts, not costed against the $14K.

Web game + gamified pre-save

The Machine Room

A browser-playable room built from the album's Blender assets (the modular platforms, facades and engine-parts Joseph is already making). Players walk and parkour through the Fantastic Machines world; billboards along the route read “new single, out now,” FIFA-pitch-side style, so it never feels like an ad over the animation. A gamified gate unlocks the next room: “your phone number is your ticket to the ride.” Email / phone drops straight into Laylo.

Why. The best answer to the core problem. It converts curiosity into an owned email / SMS list, the in-world billboards make promo immersive, and it reuses assets we're already building. Clip the playthrough for Reels and it doubles as the album's dark-ride content.
Custom microsite

Archive: the Machine repair manual

A standalone site framed as a repair manual for a machine that never existed. Each page is one component, illustrated, with a made-up technical name and an unknown function. New parts get added across the rollout; the full machine is never shown until album day. Dr. Seuss meets Looney Toons, pure cartoon absurdism.

Why. A collectible artifact fans share, the modern liner notes. Generates inbound press and “look at this” posts, and becomes the destination link from every Reel and every drop.
IRL booth

Collaborative Animation Booth

A booth at the Heavy Manners show (and adjacent animated-shorts events) where attendees draw one component of the machine on an iPad. Each drawing joins a growing master illustration; the booth prints a personalized flier with the current machine and their piece in it. By album day the machine is fully assembled, built by strangers.

Why. Turns every event into a content moment, a fan artifact, and a real piece of the album world. “Your drawing made it into the film” converts attendees into superfans.
